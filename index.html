MEDICARE BASICS

What is Medicare Part A?

Medicare Part A is Hospital Insurance. It helps cover inpatient hospital care, skilled nursing facility care, hospice care, and some home health care.

A

The hospital side of Original Medicare

Original Medicare has two main parts: Part A (Hospital Insurance) and Part B (Medical Insurance). Part A primarily helps pay for qualifying inpatient and facility-based care.

What does Part A cover?

🏥 Inpatient Hospital Care

Helps cover care when you're formally admitted to a hospital as an inpatient.

🩺 Skilled Nursing Facility Care

Helps cover qualifying short-term skilled nursing and rehabilitation care. It generally doesn't cover long-term custodial nursing-home care.

♥ Hospice Care

Helps cover hospice services for people who meet Medicare's eligibility requirements.

⌂ Home Health Care

Helps cover certain qualifying home health services when Medicare's requirements are met.

Do you pay a premium for Part A?

Most people pay $0 per month for Part A. This is called premium-free Part A. Generally, you qualify when you or your spouse paid Medicare taxes long enough while working — usually at least 10 years.

If you don't qualify for premium-free Part A, Medicare may allow you to purchase it.

Does Part A pay 100% of your hospital costs?

No. Under Original Medicare, deductibles and coinsurance can apply. Part A uses benefit periods for inpatient hospital and skilled nursing facility costs, which means the Part A deductible isn't simply a once-a-year deductible.

Easy way to remember it: Part A = Hospital Insurance. Part B = Medical Insurance, which generally covers doctors, outpatient care, durable medical equipment, and many preventive services.
Learn more at Medicare.gov

Educational information only. Medicare coverage and costs are subject to Medicare rules and may change.

MEDICARE BASICS

What is Medicare Part B?

Medicare Part B is Medical Insurance. It helps cover services from doctors and other health care providers, outpatient care, durable medical equipment, and many preventive services.

B

The medical side of Original Medicare

Original Medicare has two main parts: Part A (Hospital Insurance) and Part B (Medical Insurance). Part B generally helps pay for medically necessary doctor services, outpatient care, and many services you receive without being admitted to a hospital.

What does Part B cover?

👨‍⚕️ Doctors & Medical Services

Medically necessary services from doctors and other health care providers, including many specialist services.

🏥 Outpatient Care

Services such as outpatient procedures, X-rays, diagnostic testing, and certain hospital outpatient services.

♿ Durable Medical Equipment

Certain medically necessary equipment such as wheelchairs, walkers, and hospital beds when Medicare's requirements are met.

✓ Preventive Services

Many screenings, vaccines, and preventive services, including the “Welcome to Medicare” visit and yearly Wellness visits when eligible.

Do you pay a premium for Part B?

Yes. Part B generally has a monthly premium. The amount can depend on your income. Part B also has an annual deductible.

After the deductible, under Original Medicare you typically pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount for many covered Part B services when your provider accepts assignment. Many covered preventive services cost you nothing when the requirements are met.

Why is Part B especially important for TRICARE For Life?

For most TRICARE For Life beneficiaries, having Medicare Part A and Part B is required to remain eligible for TRICARE For Life. Medicare generally pays first for Medicare-covered services and TRICARE For Life can then pay its share for TRICARE-covered services, subject to applicable rules.

Easy way to remember it: Part A = Hospital Insurance. Part B = Medical Insurance — doctors, outpatient care, medical equipment, and many preventive services.
Learn more at Medicare.gov

Educational information only. Medicare coverage, premiums, deductibles, and coinsurance are subject to Medicare rules and may change.

MEDICARE BASICS

What is Medicare Advantage?

Medicare Advantage, also called Medicare Part C, is another way to receive your Medicare Part A and Part B benefits. These plans are offered by private insurance companies approved by Medicare.

C

An alternative way to receive your Medicare benefits

You still have Medicare when you join a Medicare Advantage plan, but most of your Part A and Part B benefits are provided through the Medicare-approved private plan instead of directly through Original Medicare.

What can a Medicare Advantage plan include?

🏥 Part A + Part B

Medicare Advantage plans must cover almost all medically necessary services that Original Medicare covers under Part A and Part B.

💊 Prescription Drug Coverage

Most Medicare Advantage plans include Medicare Part D prescription drug coverage in the same plan.

🦷 Extra Benefits

Depending on the plan, additional benefits may include dental, vision, hearing, fitness programs, and other supplemental benefits that Original Medicare doesn't normally cover.

💰 Annual Out-of-Pocket Limit

Medicare Advantage plans have a yearly limit on what you pay out of pocket for covered Part A and Part B services. The specific limit varies by plan.

Do Medicare Advantage plans have networks?

Many do. Depending on the type of plan, you may need to use doctors, hospitals, and other providers in the plan's network for non-emergency care. Some plans allow out-of-network care, usually at a higher cost.

This is why checking your doctors before choosing a plan is important. Our plan comparison process is designed to help you see which available plans work with the doctors you want to keep.

What does Medicare Advantage cost?

Costs vary by plan. Some Medicare Advantage plans have a $0 monthly plan premium, while others charge a premium. You must continue paying your Medicare Part B premium to remain enrolled in Medicare Advantage. Copayments, coinsurance, deductibles, and the plan's annual out-of-pocket maximum also vary by plan.

Medicare Advantage and TRICARE For Life

Having TRICARE For Life does not automatically prevent you from enrolling in a Medicare Advantage plan. If you enroll, you still have Medicare and must continue to meet the Medicare eligibility requirements for TRICARE For Life.

Because TRICARE For Life and Medicare Advantage can interact differently depending on the service, provider, and plan, it's especially important for veterans and military retirees to understand the plan's network, benefits, and how claims may be handled before enrolling.

Why would someone choose Medicare Advantage?

One Plan

Part A, Part B, and usually Part D can be bundled together in one plan.

Additional Benefits

Plans may offer benefits such as dental, vision, hearing, fitness, and other supplemental benefits.

Predictable Copays

Many plans use set copayments for certain services, although costs and rules vary by plan.

Out-of-Pocket Protection

Plans include an annual maximum out-of-pocket limit for covered Part A and Part B services.

Easy way to remember it: Medicare Advantage = Part C. It combines your Medicare Part A and Part B coverage through a Medicare-approved private plan, and most plans also include Part D prescription drug coverage.

MEDICARE ADVANTAGE FOR VETERANS

What are Medicare Advantage Only (MA-Only) plans?

MA-Only plans are Medicare Advantage plans that do not include Part D prescription drug coverage. They can be especially worth exploring for veterans who already have creditable prescription coverage through programs such as TRICARE For Life or VA benefits.

You don't give up your Medicare benefits.

Medicare Advantage plans must cover the medically necessary services that Original Medicare covers. The difference is that a private insurance company administers those Medicare benefits. Depending on the plan and where you live, the plan may also provide additional benefits that Original Medicare doesn't normally cover.

NEW TO MEDICARE

New to Medicare? Start Here.

Medicare can feel confusing at first. The good news is that the basics are straightforward once you understand the different parts, when to enroll, and what choices you have after Original Medicare.

First, understand the building blocks.

Original Medicare is made up of Part A and Part B. Part A is Hospital Insurance. Part B is Medical Insurance. Once you're enrolled in both, you can decide whether to stay with Original Medicare or receive your Medicare benefits through a Medicare Advantage plan.

The main parts of Medicare

A

Medicare Part A

Hospital Insurance. Helps cover inpatient hospital care, qualifying skilled nursing facility care, hospice, and some home health care.

B

Medicare Part B

Medical Insurance. Helps cover doctors, outpatient care, durable medical equipment, and many preventive services.

C

Medicare Advantage

Also called Part C. A private Medicare plan that provides your Part A and Part B benefits and may include additional benefits.

D

Medicare Part D

Prescription drug coverage. Part D can be purchased as a stand-alone plan or may be included in many Medicare Advantage plans.

When can you sign up?

Your Initial Enrollment Period

For most people, this is a 7-month period that begins 3 months before the month you turn 65, includes your birthday month, and continues for 3 months after.

If you already receive Social Security benefits when you become eligible, you may be enrolled in Medicare automatically. Other people need to sign up themselves.

What happens after you have Part A and Part B?

You generally have two main paths:

Stay with Original Medicare

You can generally use any doctor or hospital in the United States that accepts Medicare. You may also consider a Medicare Supplement policy and a Part D prescription drug plan.

Choose Medicare Advantage

You receive your Medicare benefits through a Medicare-approved private plan. These plans can include additional benefits, but many use provider networks — which is why checking your doctors is important.

If you have TRICARE For Life, CHAMPVA, or VA coverage

Your situation may be different from someone relying only on Medicare. In particular, TRICARE For Life generally works with Medicare Part A and Part B, and many veterans already have prescription coverage through military or VA benefits.

That can make certain Medicare Advantage or MA-Only options worth reviewing — but your doctors and existing coverage should be checked before making a change.

Confirm your Medicare eligibility and enrollment dates.

Know when your Part A and Part B coverage begins.

2
Review any coverage you already have.

That includes TRICARE For Life, CHAMPVA, VA benefits, employer coverage, or retiree coverage.

3
Check your doctors.

If you're considering Medicare Advantage, make sure the doctors you want to keep participate in the plan's network.

4
Compare available plans and benefits.

Then choose the option that best fits your doctors, costs, and coverage needs.

MEDICARE BASICS

Understanding Medicare Enrollment Periods

Medicare has specific times when you can first enroll, change plans, or make certain coverage changes. Knowing which enrollment period applies to you can help you avoid delays, coverage gaps, or possible late-enrollment penalties.

The most important rule

Your enrollment window depends on where you are in your Medicare journey. Someone turning 65 for the first time has different options than someone who already has Medicare and wants to change plans.

Initial Enrollment Period (IEP)

IEP

Your first opportunity to enroll in Medicare

For most people, your Initial Enrollment Period is a 7-month window that begins 3 months before the month you turn 65, includes the month you turn 65, and continues for 3 months after.

3 months beforeBirthday month3 months after

If you qualify for Medicare due to disability or another circumstance, your timing may be different.

Annual Enrollment Period (AEP)

AEP

October 15 – December 7

This is the main annual period when people with Medicare can review and make changes to their coverage for the following year.

  • Switch from Original Medicare to a Medicare Advantage plan.
  • Switch from Medicare Advantage back to Original Medicare.
  • Change from one Medicare Advantage plan to another.
  • Join, switch, or drop a Part D prescription drug plan.

Changes made during AEP generally take effect January 1 of the following year.

Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period (MA OEP)

OEP

January 1 – March 31

If you're already enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan, this period gives you another opportunity to make one change.

  • Switch to a different Medicare Advantage plan.
  • Leave Medicare Advantage and return to Original Medicare.
  • If you return to Original Medicare, you may also be able to join a stand-alone Part D plan.

You generally cannot use this period to move from Original Medicare into Medicare Advantage.

Special Enrollment Periods (SEPs)

SEP

Life events can create additional enrollment opportunities.

Special Enrollment Periods can let you enroll or make changes outside the standard annual windows when certain events happen.

  • You move and your current plan is no longer available or new plan options become available.
  • You lose employer, retiree, Medicaid, or other qualifying coverage.
  • You move into, live in, or move out of certain institutions.
  • Your plan ends its contract with Medicare or significantly changes its service area.
  • You qualify for certain assistance programs or other Medicare-approved circumstances.

The exact timing and choices depend on the reason for the SEP.

General Enrollment Period (GEP)

GEP

January 1 – March 31

If you didn't sign up for Medicare Part A and/or Part B when you were first eligible and you don't qualify for a Special Enrollment Period, the General Enrollment Period may allow you to enroll.

Late-enrollment penalties can apply in some situations, so it's important to understand why you delayed enrollment and whether you had qualifying coverage.

What if you have TRICARE For Life, CHAMPVA, or VA coverage?

Your military or veteran coverage can affect what Medicare choices make sense, but it doesn't eliminate Medicare's enrollment rules. For example, TRICARE For Life generally requires Medicare Part A and Part B once you're eligible for Medicare based on age.

If you're considering a Medicare Advantage or MA-Only plan, the enrollment period still matters — and so does checking your doctors before you change coverage.

Not sure which enrollment period applies to you?

Start with your Medicare status, your current coverage, and whether you've had a recent life event. From there, we can help narrow down which enrollment opportunities may apply.

Learn more at Medicare.gov

Educational information only. Enrollment periods and eligibility rules can vary based on your circumstances. Always confirm the enrollment period that applies to you before making a coverage change.

MEDICARE + MILITARY COVERAGE

How Medicare Works With TRICARE For Life

TRICARE For Life works alongside Medicare for eligible military retirees and family members. Understanding which coverage pays first — and what happens if you choose Medicare Advantage — can make your Medicare decisions much easier.

★

You can have a Medicare Advantage plan and TRICARE For Life together.

You keep Medicare Part A and Part B, and your Medicare Advantage plan provides those benefits. The Medicare Advantage plan generally pays first for Medicare-covered services, and TRICARE For Life generally pays second for TRICARE-covered services.

Why are Medicare Part A and Part B important?

A

Part A — Hospital Insurance

Helps cover inpatient hospital care and certain other qualifying services.

B

Part B — Medical Insurance

Helps cover doctors, outpatient care, medical equipment, and many preventive services.

What about prescription drugs?

TRICARE For Life beneficiaries generally have prescription coverage through the TRICARE Pharmacy Program, including options such as military pharmacies and TRICARE Pharmacy Home Delivery. Because you may already have creditable drug coverage, you may not need a Medicare Part D plan.

This is one reason MA-Only Medicare Advantage plans can be worth exploring for some veterans.

Can I enroll in Medicare Advantage and keep TRICARE For Life?

YES

Enrolling in Medicare Advantage does not automatically make you lose TRICARE For Life.

A Medicare Advantage plan is another way to receive your Medicare Part A and Part B benefits. You still have Medicare and must continue meeting Medicare and TRICARE For Life eligibility requirements.

However, Medicare Advantage plans can have their own provider networks and plan rules. That's where choosing the right plan becomes important.

The key question: Do your doctors work with the plan?

For many veterans, this is the most important part of comparing Medicare Advantage plans. Extra benefits aren't very useful if the civilian doctors you want to keep don't participate in the plan's network.

That’s why our process checks your doctors before showing you plan options. We help identify Medicare Advantage plans that include the doctors you want to keep, then compare the additional benefits each plan offers. This helps you find a plan that works with your doctors while giving you access to benefits beyond Original Medicare.

Why MA-Only Plans Can Be a Great Fit With TRICARE For Life

TRICARE For Life already includes prescription drug coverage through the TRICARE Pharmacy Program. That means you may not need prescription drug coverage included in your Medicare Advantage plan.

MA-Only plans are designed without Part D prescription coverage and may offer additional benefits such as dental, vision, hearing, fitness benefits, and even a reduction in your Medicare Part B premium, depending on the plan.
